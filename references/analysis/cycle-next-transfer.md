The new observable is the **global maximum velocity magnitude** after

There is one fixed sufficiently short interval, uniform for normalized
viscosity \(0\le\mu\le1\), on which

\[
 \|U_\mu(\tau)\|_\infty^2
       \le3-3\mu\tau-\frac45\tau^4,
       \qquad0<\tau\le T_* .                     \tag{1}
\]

For suitably small positive \(\mu\), total enstrophy nevertheless

actual finite transfer can increase gradients while **decreasing**
maximum speed. It does not satisfy a stage requirement of amplified
maximum velocity on this interval. Other observables, later times,

## 1. The datum, pressure and exact finite Fourier calculation

On the torus of side \(2\pi\), let

\[
 \partial_\tau U_\mu+(U_\mu\cdot\nabla)U_\mu+\nabla\Pi_\mu
       =\mu\Delta U_\mu,\quad\nabla\cdot U_\mu=0,
 \qquad U_\mu(0)=(\sin y,\sin z,\sin x).           \tag{2}
\]

Pressure has zero spatial mean. Write \(U_E=U_{\mu=0}\) only for the
auxiliary smooth Euler solution used in a uniform short-time comparison;
the conclusion (1) applies to positive viscosity. The known first jets are

\[
 U_{E,1}=-N,\quad U_{E,2}=T,\quad
 N=(\sin z\cos y,\sin x\cos z,\sin y\cos x),
\]
\[
 T=-(\sin y\sin^2z,\sin z\sin^2x,\sin x\sin^2y),
 \quad\Pi_{E,0}=0,\quad
 \Pi_{E,1}=-2\cos x\cos y\cos z.                 \tag{3}
\]

Here subscripts after the comma mean time derivatives at zero. The
second pressure jet is the earlier \(-\frac43F\), with \(F\) defined
in the point-pressure note. Subsequent jets are obtained from the
**full** periodic Leray projection. No shell is suppressed.

For a precise reproducible algebra specification, write the Fourier
coefficients of each real odd velocity jet as
\(\widehat U_{E,j}(k)=i v_j(k)\), with rational real vectors \(v_j\).
Define

\[
 R_n(m)=\sum_{j=0}^n{n\choose j}
       \sum_{k+l=m}(l\cdot v_j(k))v_{n-j}(l).
\]

For every nonzero \(m\), the exact momentum and pressure recurrences are

\[
 v_{n+1}(m)=R_n(m)-m\frac{m\cdot R_n(m)}{|m|^2},
 \qquad \widehat\Pi_{E,n}(m)=\frac{m\cdot R_n(m)}{|m|^2}.      \tag{4}
\]

The zero velocity mode remains zero and pressure's zero mode is fixed
to zero. The [exact control](checks/check-cycle-next-transfer.py)
uses rational coefficients and retains all modes generated by (4).
The nonzero velocity-mode counts through derivative order four are
\(6,12,18,48,90\). This is differentiation of the PDE at its datum,
not time stepping of a finite-mode replacement for the PDE.

## 2. A fixed-point calculation misses the moving maximum

Let \(G_E(\tau,x)=|U_E(\tau,x)|^2\). Its initial global maximum is 3,
attained at the eight nondegenerate points

\[
 p_\sigma=\frac\pi2(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3),\quad
 \sigma_j\in\{-1,1\},\qquad
 v_\sigma=U_E(0,p_\sigma)=(\sigma_2,\sigma_3,\sigma_1).
\]

Put \(G_j=\partial_\tau^jG_E(0,\cdot)\). Equations (3) give

\[
 G_0=|U_0|^2,\quad G_1=-2U_0\cdot N,
 \quad G_2=2|N|^2+2U_0\cdot T.
\]

At each \(p=p_\sigma\), with \(v=v_\sigma\), one finds exactly

\[
 \nabla G_0=0,\quad D^2G_0=-2I,\quad D^3G_0=0,
 \quad \nabla G_1=2v,\quad D^2G_1=0,\quad\nabla G_2=0.       \tag{5}
\]

The implicit-function theorem therefore supplies a unique nearby
maximizing branch \(p(\tau)\), with

\[
 p(\tau)=p+\tau v+O(\tau^3).                    \tag{6}
\]

There is no quadratic displacement: substituting a branch
\(p+\tau v+\tau^2w\) into \(\nabla G_E=0\) makes the first-order
terms cancel and leaves \(-2w\) at second order by (5).
Away from small neighborhoods of the eight initial maxima, compactness
gives a strict initial gap below 3. Uniform continuity preserves that
gap on a short common interval. Every global maximum is consequently
one of these eight branches. The local Hessians stay negative definite.

At a fixed initial point \(p\), the Eulerian second derivative of
\(G_E\) equals \(-6\). Its apparent quadratic loss is cancelled by
moving the maximizer: the Hessian optimization contributes \(+6\).
The actual global-speed question is therefore not settled at second
order. The inverse error also occurs: freezing just \(U_0-\tau N\)
would give a spurious optimized increase \(3+3\tau^2+O(\tau^3)\).
Its omitted second-order nonlinear and pressure response matters.

## 3. The complete subsequent response gives a quartic loss

Evaluation of (4) along each line \(p+\tau v\) gives

\[
 U_E(\tau,p+\tau v)
     =v-\frac4{15}\tau^4v+O(\tau^5).             \tag{7}
\]

The first, second and third coefficients along this line vanish.
All eight sign choices give the same magnitude coefficient. Its
pressure content can be displayed separately. The three contributions
to the order-\(\tau^3\) pressure gradient along this line are

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{term in the full pressure Taylor expansion}&
       [\tau^3],\nabla\Pi_E(\tau,p+\tau v)\\ \hline
 \tau\Pi_{E,1}&2v\\
 \frac{\tau^2}{2}\Pi_{E,2}&-\frac43v\\
 \frac{\tau^3}{6}\Pi_{E,3}&\frac25v
 \end{array}
 \qquad 2-\frac43+\frac25=\frac{16}{15}.          \tag{8}
\]

In particular \(\nabla\Pi_{E,3}(p)=\frac{12}{5}v\).
These are pressure jets of the full solution, including all generated
wave interactions in (4). They are not obtained from the central
gradient \(B(\tau,0)\) alone. Along the corresponding fluid particle,
the first possible pressure acceleration is
\(-\frac{16}{15}\tau^3v\), agreeing with the velocity coefficient
\(-\frac4{15}\tau^4v\) in (7). The particle and the straight line
agree to the orders used here; alternatively (7) follows directly from
the velocity jets without that observation.

By (5), the spatial gradient of \(G_E\) at \(p+\tau v\) is
\(O(\tau^3)\). By (6), the distance to the true maximizing point is
\(O(\tau^3)\). Their values of \(G_E\) thus differ by \(O(\tau^6)\).
Taking the maximum over all eight branches in (7) yields

\[
 M_E(\tau):=\|U_E(\tau)\|_\infty^2
      =3-\frac85\tau^4+O(\tau^5).                \tag{9}
\]

This calculation settles one actual gain requirement: the first
nonzero inviscid response of the global speed maximum is a loss.
It does not give a maximum principle for general three-dimensional
Euler or NS flows, nor a sign for this observable at arbitrary later time.

## 4. Uniform remainders and positive viscosity

All estimates here use the full actual smooth solutions. The fixed
trigonometric datum lies in every Sobolev space. Choose H20 on the
fixed torus. The usual differentiated energy inequality gives constants
\(T_0>0,M<\infty\), independent of \(0\le\mu\le1\), for which

\[
 \sup_{[0,T_0]}\|U_\mu\|_{H^{20}}\le M.          \tag{10}
\]

The viscous term is nonpositive in this energy bound. The same local
bound applies to Euler. Differentiating the exact projected equation
successively gives, for \(0\le j\le5\),

\[
 \sup_{[0,T_0]}\|\partial_\tau^jU_\mu\|_{H^{20-2j}}
          \le C_j(M).                            \tag{11}
\]

The lowest space here is H10, which controls the spatial derivatives
needed for Taylor expansion and for the maximizing-branch argument.
Equations (10)–(11), pressure's exact Sobolev mapping and Taylor's
integral remainder justify the uniform remainders used in (5)–(9).
There is a fixed constant \(C_E\) such that
\(|M_E-3+\frac85\tau^4|\le C_E\tau^5\) on a shorter common interval.

For clarity, viscosity is controlled to its **first time order**; an
unsigned \(O(\mu)\) error would mask the quartic effect. Let
\(W=U_\mu-U_E\). Its exact divergence-free difference equation is

\[
 \partial_\tau W+\mathbb P[(U_E\cdot\nabla)W
       +(W\cdot\nabla)U_E+(W\cdot\nabla)W]
       -\mu\Delta W=\mu\Delta U_E,\quad W(0)=0.  \tag{12}
\]

With (10), an H16 difference-energy estimate gives
\(\|W(\tau)\|_{H^{16}}\le C\mu\tau\). This follows also by writing
the advecting coefficient as \(U_\mu\); all coefficients are uniformly
bounded by (10). The equation then implies in H14

\[
 \|\partial_\tau W-\mu\Delta U_0\|_{H^{14}}
       \le C\mu\tau.
\]

Indeed every term containing \(W\) is \(O(\mu\tau)\),
\(\mu\Delta W=O(\mu^2\tau)\), and
\(\mu\Delta(U_E-U_0)=O(\mu\tau)\). Since \(\Delta U_0=-U_0\),
integration gives the uniform estimate

\[
 U_\mu(\tau,x)=U_E(\tau,x)-\mu\tau U_0(x)
                  +O_{C^2}(\mu\tau^2).           \tag{13}
\]

As \(U_E-U_0=O_{C^2}(\tau)\), this implies

\[
 |U_\mu|^2=(1-2\mu\tau)|U_E|^2+O_{C^0}(\mu\tau^2).        \tag{14}
\]

For \(\tau\le1/4\), the multiplier is positive. Taking the global
maximum in (14) and then applying (9) proves

\[
 M_\mu(\tau)=3-6\mu\tau-\frac85\tau^4
                   +O(\tau^5+\mu\tau^2),         \tag{15}
\]

uniformly for \(0\le\mu\le1\). In particular there are fixed
\(C_E,C_V\) such that the absolute remainder in (15) is at most
\(C_E\tau^5+C_V\mu\tau^2\), after enlarging the constants finitely.
Choose the common lifespan shorter if necessary and set

\[
 0<T_*\le\min\left\{T_0,\frac14,
          \frac4{5(1+C_E)},\frac3{1+C_V}\right\}.             \tag{16}
\]

This makes the two errors at most \(\frac45\tau^4\) and
\(3\mu\tau\), respectively, and proves (1) on the entire stated
positive-time interval. All constants come from fixed local estimates;
no numerically optimized interval length is asserted.

## 5. Simultaneous enstrophy gain and the physical meaning

The reviewed enstrophy result supplies

\[
 \frac{Q_\mu(\tau)}{Q_0}
  =1-2\mu\tau+(\tfrac12+2\mu^2)\tau^2+R_Q,
 \quad |R_Q|\le C_Q\tau^3,
 \quad Q_0=\frac34(2\pi)^3.                       \tag{17}
\]

Choose one fixed
\(0<\tau_\dagger\le\min\{T_*,1/(4C_Q),1\}\)
inside that earlier estimate's lifespan, and choose
\(0<\mu\le\tau_\dagger/16\). Then the earlier margin and (1) hold
for the **same solution at the same time**:

\[
 Q_\mu(\tau_\dagger)\ge Q_0(1+\tau_\dagger^2/8),
 \qquad
 \|U_\mu(\tau_\dagger)\|_\infty^2
      \le3-3\mu\tau_\dagger-\tfrac45\tau_\dagger^4<3.       \tag{18}
\]

Return to the original positive physical viscosity \(\nu\), with
\(u(t,x)=A U_\mu(Aqt,qx)\), integer \(q\ge1\),
\(\mu=\nu q/A\). The speed-squared inequality is multiplied by
\(A^2\), enstrophy by \(A^2q^2\), and the observation time is
\(t_\dagger=\tau_\dagger/(Aq)\). Taking
\(A\ge16\nu q/\tau_\dagger\) preserves strictly positive viscosity.
Each fixed \(A,q\) is one actual smooth datum. Varying them does not
produce an infinite trajectory or a terminal smooth forcing construction.

The reverse wave is actually generated and participates in the full
subsequent response. Its reuse does not, on this interval, amplify the
global velocity maximum above the supplied datum's maximum. Its
gradient redistribution remains useful, as (18) quantifies. A next
proposal requiring maximum-speed growth must identify a later interval
or another verified gain mechanism and account for the resulting full
state. Nothing here forbids later growth or a differently stated transfer.
