# Origin and credits

**Hunter Bown** initiated and directed Transformatics while studying the
Navier–Stokes equations with AI tools. The project developed into a proposed way
of organizing finite transformations, models, and predictions, with worked
examples and research case studies.

Hunter describes the collaboration as involving frontier and open-source models,
especially **DeepSeek and GLM**. Other assistance included Claude, Cursor, and
Codex for drafting, calculations, review, and implementation.

## Mathematical sources

The foundations belong to established mathematics: finite differences, pullbacks,
Koopman operators, Lie derivatives, dynamical systems, Fourier analysis, and
numerical approximation. The [literature guide](references.md) identifies these
sources. The fluid chapters cite the PDE and compact-Euler-profile results they use.

**OpenAI** authored the external forced Navier–Stokes construction and the forced
computation result discussed in the films. Credit those results to their original
authors. Transformatics did not solve the three-dimensional unforced problem.

Lean, Mathlib, KaTeX, MathJax, Playwright, FFmpeg, and the numerical and speech
libraries used by the examples are credited in their source and build instructions.
The films use synthetic Microsoft speech voices.
